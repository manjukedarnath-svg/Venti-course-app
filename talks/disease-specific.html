The table is the floor. Then titrate.

PEEP
01

Three things at once. Oxygenation, haemodynamics and static compliance, never one in isolation (CR 3.4.1).

02

Compliance is the signal. Rising with PEEP means recruitment; falling means overdistension. Driving pressure tells you the same.

03

Look at the lung. Ultrasound shows collapse and predicts the response to PEEP and proning.

04

Stepwise, not sustained. Slow incremental and decremental PEEP steps may be used; sustained inflations cannot be recommended (CR 3.6).

05

Three correlations. PEEP against the OI; PEEP against the stress index on the pressure–time curve; PEEP against mechanical power, with the driving pressure in mind.

PALICC-2 · Kedarnath, Ventilating a child with ARDS · Tusman, Crit Ultrasound J 2016

Find the PEEP on the way down

Recruitment manoeuvre
01
PrepareSedated, no spontaneous breaths. PC with fixed ΔP ≈ 15, rate 30, I:E 1:1, FiO₂ 1.0. In-line suction only; never disconnect.
02
ClimbPEEP up 2–3 cmH₂O every 2 min. Watch dynamic compliance: the PEEP with the highest Cdyn is the critical opening pressure.
03
DescendPEEP down in the same steps. The highest Cdyn on the way down, just before it falls, marks the closing pressure.
04
Re-open and setOne more step to the opening pressure, then PEEP = closing pressure + 2 cmH₂O.
05
Stop rulesSpO₂ < 88, HR or BP moving, arrhythmia, any air leak. Not with pneumothorax, blebs, an unstable circulation or raised ICP.
06
Adult alternativeChang / Girgis: CPAP 40 for 40 s, then PEEP 20 stepped down 2 every 15–20 min until SpO₂ < 90; optimum is the step before.
Boriosi et al, Pediatr Crit Care Med 2011;12:431–436 · Chang ch. 15, table 15-4 · PALICC-2 CR 3.6

01

Airway opening pressure first. Low-flow inflation (≤ 6 L/min) from PEEP 5; AOP is where volume starts to rise. If AOP > 5, use AOP as the low PEEP.

02

Compliance at low PEEP. Volume control, passive patient, no leak. Crs,low = VT ÷ (Pplat − PEEP).

03

One-breath PEEP drop. Ventilate at PEEP 15, then drop to 5 in a single breath and read the exhaled volume of that breath.

04

Subtract the inflation. Released = exhaled − VT. Expected from inflation alone = Crs,low × ΔPEEP. Recruited volume = released − expected.

05

The ratio. Crec = recruited ÷ ΔPEEP. R/I = Crec ÷ Crs,low.

R/I ≥ 0.5 · recruitable

Higher PEEP opens lung. Go up, within plateau, ΔP and blood pressure.

R/I < 0.5 · poor recruiter

PEEP mostly inflates what is open. Keep it lower; prone instead.

Why is the oxygen not going in?

Refractory hypoxaemia
01

Shunt or dead space? AVDSf = (PaCO₂ − EtCO₂) ÷ PaCO₂. Easy at the bedside; tracks severity and the response to proning.

02

Recruitable? R/I ratio ≥ 0.5, collapse on lung ultrasound, compliance that rises with PEEP. If not recruitable, PEEP only overdistends.

03

The heart? Echo for the right ventricle: high pressures and hypercapnia squeeze it. Fluid balance, pulmonary hypertension, iNO if it is there.

04

Hypoxaemia or hypoxia? SpO₂ 88 with normal lactate and perfusion is a lower-risk place than a plateau of 32. Judge between the two injuries.

Bhalla et al, dead space and mortality in paediatric AHRF · Yehya, AVDSf at onset and 24 h · PALICC-2 CR 2.1 · Kedarnath, Ventilating a child with ARDS

Oscillate on the open lung

HFOV
01

Six mechanisms. Bulk convection, pendelluft, Taylor dispersion, asymmetric velocity profiles, cardiogenic mixing, molecular diffusion.

02

The safe window. A stable mean airway pressure between the zone of atelectrauma and the zone of volutrauma; APRV aims for the same window from the other side.

03

Open-lung approach. Step mean airway pressure up, then down, to find the lung volume (de Jager). Transcutaneous CO₂, SpO₂ and blood pressure continuously.

04

Frequency first. The highest frequency you can, irrespective of amplitude: high frequency means low strain per oscillation.

05

PALICC-2. No recommendation for or against; may be considered when goals are not met on protective conventional ventilation (CR 3.8).

de Jager et al, open-lung HFOV in children · Harcourt et al, PCCM 2014: oscillators differ, know your machine · PALICC-2

Refer early. Rest the lung.

ECMO
01

When. Protective strategies give inadequate gas exchange in reversible disease. Serial expert evaluation, not a single number (PALICC-2 Sec 8).

02

Which. VV when cardiac function is adequate; VA in small infants. Transfer early if you are not an ECMO centre.

03

On the circuit. Normal PaO₂, not hyperoxia · slow PaCO₂ reduction · rest ventilation within protective limits · ultrasound follow-up of the lung.

04

Evidence. CESAR: 63% vs 47% disability-free survival at 6 months. EOLIA: 60-day mortality 35% vs 46%, p 0.09, 28% crossover.

05

Prevention. LOVS: open-lung PEEP halved refractory hypoxaemia (4.6% vs 10.2%) and death with it (4.2% vs 8.9%); hospital mortality 36% vs 40%, not significant.

PALICC-2 Section 8 · Peek et al, CESAR, Lancet 2009 · Combes et al, EOLIA, NEJM 2018 · Meade et al, LOVS, JAMA 2008 · Kedarnath, Ventilating a child with ARDS

Expiration tells you first

What the screen shows
Normal

Tall peak, short tail, back to zero with time to spare.

Obstructed

Each breath leaves a little behind

Dynamic hyperinflation

Slow expiratory flow, short expiratory time and high minute ventilation each raise the end-expiratory volume.

The pressure left in the alveoli at end-expiration is auto-PEEP. The ventilator does not show it unless you ask.

Costs: dead space and CO₂, missed triggers and work, low venous return and hypotension, air leak.

J Pediatr Crit Care 2015;2(1):55–66 · Tuxen

Hold the breath, read the number

Measuring hyperinflation
01

Expiratory hold. Relaxed patient. Pressure above set PEEP is auto-PEEP. Total PEEP = set + auto.

02

Inspiratory hold, 0.4 s. Peak falls to plateau. Plateau is the pressure that injures; rising plateau with unchanged compliance = trapping.

03

End-inspiratory volume. All the gas exhaled in a 20–60 s apnoea. Above 20 mL/kg predicts hypotension and air leak.

04

Occult auto-PEEP. Plugged units do not report. Low measured auto-PEEP with a high plateau and a hyperinflated film: trust the plateau.

J Pediatr Crit Care 2015;2(1):55–66 · Tuxen & Lane 1987

B. The plateau is low, so the lung is compliant. The 22 cmH₂O gap is resistive: tube, secretions or bronchospasm. A pneumothorax would raise the plateau; a leak would stop volume returning to baseline.

Asthma: the settings

A clinical decision, not a gas

When to intubate
01

Arrest or near-arrest. Cardiorespiratory arrest, respiratory arrest, profound bradypnoea.

02

Exhaustion. Rising PaCO₂ and acidosis despite maximal bronchodilators, magnesium and steroids.

03

Falling conscious level. Drowsy, cannot protect the airway, cannot take the therapy.

04

Not on its own. Hypercapnia alone is not an indication. Severe hypoxaemia despite oxygen is rarely the reason.

05

Do not delay. Once deemed necessary, delay reduces the chance of a good outcome.

A physiologically difficult airway

Before the tube
01

Fluid first. Dehydrated and preload-dependent; positive pressure will cut venous return. Bolus before induction, vasopressor drawn up.

02

Ketamine. 1–2 mg/kg. Bronchodilator, keeps the blood pressure. Add a paralytic for a clean first pass.

03

Largest cuffed tube. Resistance falls with the fourth power of the radius; a big tube clears secretions and takes high pressures.

04

Most experienced hands. Orotracheal, modified rapid sequence. Expect the gas exchange to worsen for a while.

05

Bag slowly. Very slow rates, long expiration, pause between breaths. Watch for a pneumothorax.

01

Paralysed, controlled: zero. The lung is already over-inflated; most reviews recommend no PEEP during controlled ventilation.

Breathing: match it. Auto-PEEP 6, trigger 1 below PEEP: the child must pull 8 cmH₂O to trigger. External PEEP 5 brings that to 2.

Stay under the auto-PEEP. External PEEP below 75–85% of the measured auto-PEEP does not worsen hyperinflation.

Three things to keep

Takeaways
1The ARDS lung is small, not stiff. Scale the breath to the open lung: driving pressure ≤ 15.
2The asthmatic lung is full. Give it time: low rate, long expiration, and watch the flow reach zero.
3Decide which lung you have today. Bronchiolitis changes in three days; the weak child has a normal lung and needs the airway and the cough looked after.
