Is this PMV?

Check your understanding

A 9-month-old was intubated for 15 days, then extubated to BiPAP for 20 hours a day for the next 9 days. Does she meet the definition of prolonged ventilation?

ANo: only 15 days were invasive
BYes: 24 days, at least 6 h a day
CNo: BiPAP is not ventilation
DOnly if she is re-intubated

Is this PMV?

Answer
Why

Invasive and non-invasive days add together. Twenty-four consecutive days at six hours or more is prolonged ventilation, and she now needs a long-term plan.

ANo: only 15 days were invasive
BYes: 24 days, at least 6 h a day

Answer
Why

A minute into the valve

Check your understanding

A speaking valve goes on, and within a minute the child is distressed: the chest rises but no air seems to come out. Most likely cause?

AThe cuff is still inflated
BThe tube is too large
CThe valve is too small
DAnxiety

A minute into the valve

Answer
Why

With the cuff up and a one-way valve on, air goes in and cannot leave. Take the valve off at once, then deflate the cuff fully before trying again.

AThe cuff is still inflated
BThe tube is too large
CThe valve is too small

Pauses in REM

Check your understanding

A 6-year-old with Duchenne on bilevel S mode at night desaturates in REM sleep; the flow trace shows pauses with no machine breaths. What change?

ARaise EPAP
BST, backup near his own rate
CRaise IPAP only
DAdd oxygen

Pauses in REM

Answer
Why

In S mode the machine waits for a trigger. In REM sleep his weak muscles do not trigger, so nothing happens. A backup rate breathes for him when he does not.

ARaise EPAP
BST, backup near his own rate
CRaise IPAP only

Ready for home?

Check your understanding

Stable on the home ventilator for two weeks. The mother is fully trained and has done rooming-in. The father works away and has not been trained. Ready for discharge?

AYes: one trained parent is enough
BNo: second carer, 24/7 awake care
CYes, if an oximeter is supplied
DNo: must be off the vent by day

Ready for home?

Answer
Why

One carer cannot stay awake and attentive every hour of every day. The ATS recommends at least two trained family caregivers and a trained carer awake at all times.

AYes: one trained parent is enough
BNo: second carer, 24/7 awake care
CYes, if an oximeter is supplied

Straight after a routine tube change a 3-year-old desaturates. Bagging is stiff, there is no EtCO₂ trace, and the neck is swelling and crackling. Best action?

After the change

Answer
Why

No CO₂, stiff bagging and surgical emphysema after a change is a false passage. Each breath makes it worse: take the tube out, oxygenate from above, then reinsert smaller over a catheter or intubate orally.
