Numbers say what. Shapes say why.

Introduction

A peak pressure of 35 tells you something is wrong. The shape of the trace tells you whether it is the airway, the lung or the child.

In children, patient–ventilator asynchrony is seen in about one third of breaths, and it is linked to longer ventilation and longer stay.

Emrath, Curr Pediatr Rep 2021

Part 1 · 8 min

What is on the screen

Three lines, always the same order

Scalars

Pressure and flow are measured. Volume is calculated: it is the area under the flow curve.

Above the baseline is inspiration. Below it, expiration.

Time runs left to right on all three. Read them together, top to bottom, one breath at a time.

Emrath 2021 · Restrepo, Ind J Resp Care 2014

Two loops: one variable against another

Loops

Pressure–volume: pressure on x, volume on y. Its slope is compliance.

Flow–volume: volume on x, flow on y. Its expiratory limb shows obstruction.

Loops show nothing the scalars do not. They make some changes easier to see. Check which way up your machine draws flow.

Emrath 2021 · Restrepo 2014

Pressure control: flow is the answer

Flow patterns

Pressure is square. Flow starts high and decays as the lung fills and the gradient falls.

Fast decay: stiff lung or high resistance reached the target quickly. Slow decay: a long time constant.

Same pressure, different lung, different volume. Watch VT whenever compliance changes.

Same volume, different pressures

Constant vs decelerating
Constant flow · VC

Higher peak pressure, lower mean airway pressure.

Decelerating · PC

Lower peak, higher mean airway pressure. Better oxygenation in older studies; not confirmed in recent ARDS data.

Is the inspiratory time right?

Flow patterns · PC
Too long

Flow hits zero early. Nothing more goes in. Shorten Ti and give the time to expiration.

About right

Flow just reaches zero as inspiration ends.

Too short

Still flowing when it cycles. A longer Ti would add volume.

A fixed flow can starve a hungry child

Flow patterns

In volume control the flow is fixed. If the child wants more, the pressure trace is pulled down: a scooped, concave shape.

Fix: raise the flow or shorten Ti, or switch to a pressure mode where flow follows demand.

Part 4 · 8 min

Expiration and auto-PEEP

Start below the baseline

Expiration

Passive expiration is the lung emptying itself: driven by plateau minus PEEP, slowed by resistance.

It empties exponentially. τ = R × C. About 63% leaves in one τ, 95% in three.

Long tail (τ > 0.7 s, flow > 2.5 s): obstruction or high compliance. Short and tall (τ < 0.5 s): stiff lung.

Flynn et al, Anesthesiology 2022 (adult values)

Obstruction: low peak, long tail

Expiration
Normal

Sharp peak, back to zero well before the next breath.

Bronchospasm

Low peak, slow return. Air trapping risk.

After bronchodilator

Peak rises, return is quicker.

If flow does not reach zero, the lung did not empty

Auto-PEEP

The next breath starts while gas is still leaving. That trapped gas is auto-PEEP.

The flow trace shows it is there. Only an expiratory hold tells you how much: total PEEP − set PEEP.

Causes: high resistance, high rate, long Ti, large VT.

Restrepo 2014 · Flynn 2022

Give it time to empty

Auto-PEEP
Before · RR 24

Flow never reaches zero.

After · RR 12, shorter Ti

Flow reaches zero before the next breath.

A spike, then a slow tail: flow limitation

Expiration

Two compartments empty at different speeds: a fast central spike, then a slow tail from collapsing small airways.

Unlike simple auto-PEEP, it does not improve with more expiratory time.

External PEEP may hold the small airways open.

Flynn et al, Anesthesiology 2022

A beak means overdistension

Loops · P–V

At the top of the loop pressure keeps rising but volume barely does: the curve flattens into a beak.

The lung is past its upper inflection point. Reduce the tidal volume, or the PEEP.

It is the loop version of a stress index above one.

Emrath 2021 · Flynn 2022

Effort without a breath

Asynchrony · trigger

A bump on expiratory flow toward zero, a small dip in pressure, and no breath.

Usual cause: auto-PEEP the child cannot overcome, or too much support.

Fix: lower the support or Ti, add external PEEP to offset auto-PEEP, make the trigger more sensitive.

Emrath 2021 · Flynn 2022

A breath without an effort

Asynchrony · trigger

Breaths at irregular intervals with no dip before them.

In infants: a leak around an uncuffed tube looks like inspiratory flow. Cardiac oscillations and water in the circuit do the same.

Fix: the leak, the water, then a less sensitive trigger or leak compensation.

Emrath 2021

Two breaths, no exhalation in between

Asynchrony · trigger

The child is still pulling when the breath ends, so a second breath is triggered at once. Volume stacks.

Fix: match the child's neural time. Longer Ti, or a larger VT if appropriate.

Reverse triggering looks similar: the machine breath triggers the diaphragm.

Emrath 2021

Flow: hungry for more than you give

Asynchrony · flow
Volume control · starved

Fixed flow, strong effort: the pressure curve scoops down during inspiration.

Pressure control · fixed

Same child: flow rises to meet demand, pressure stays flat.

FixMore flow or faster rise · or a pressure mode, but watch VT when effort is strong

Ending too early, or too late

Asynchrony · cycle
Premature cycling

The breath ends while the child still pulls: flow bends up early in expiration. Lower the cycle-off %, lengthen Ti.

Delayed cycling

The child starts to exhale against the breath: a pressure spike at the end. Raise the cycle-off %, shorten Ti.

Pressure support, too little or too much

Asynchrony · PS
PS too low

Small breaths, fast rate, hard work. Raise PS until VT is 5–7 mL/kg and the rate settles.

PS too high

Big, long breaths and efforts that fail to trigger. Lower PS.

What changes in a small patient

Paediatrics
01

Tiny volumes. Prefer a proximal flow sensor; the circuit can absorb much of a neonatal breath.

02

Uncuffed tubes leak. Volume never closes, triggers become unreliable.

03

Fast rates, short Te. In obstructive bronchiolitis τ can approach 1 s: auto-PEEP comes fast.

04

Noisy signals. Cardiac oscillations and secretions are easy to mistake for effort.

A 4-month-old who never empties

Bedside case
RSV · 5 kg · PC 20/5 · RR 30 · Ti 0.5 s

Expiratory flow never reaches zero. VT is about 17 mL, 3 mL/kg.

What would you change?

Lower the rate, lengthen Ti, accept the CO₂. Then look again.

Three things to keep

Takeaways
1What you set is perfectly shaped. What the lung returns is curved. Read that.
2Peak minus plateau is the airway. Plateau minus PEEP is the lung.
3If expiratory flow does not reach zero, find out why before the next breath.
