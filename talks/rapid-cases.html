Aarav is obstructing and has pink frothy secretions two hours after adenotonsillectomy. SpO₂ 84% on 10 L. What first?

ATurn the oxygen up and observe
BCPAP: head up, NPA, positive pressure
CNaloxone, then watch

After the tonsils

Answer
Why

Upper-airway obstruction plus pink froth is negative-pressure pulmonary oedema. Positive pressure treats both: it splints the airway and pushes fluid back. More oxygen only hides a rising CO₂; naloxone and furosemide are adjuncts, not the fix.

Answer
Why

The bicarbonate says chronic hypoventilation, now acute on chronic, and the oxygen made it worse. Ventilate with bilevel NIV, give only the oxygen he needs, and clear the secretions with cough assist. Intubate if NIV fails or bulbar function goes.

The bell-shaped chest

Answer
Why

The SMA consensus: bilevel NIV and mechanical insufflation-exsufflation are the primary tools; oral suction for a weak cough; never oxygen in place of ventilation. Avoid fasting, keep her hydrated, and agree the intubation threshold with the family on admission.

The falling VC

Answer
Why

A falling VC below 20 mL/kg, a MIP weaker than −30 and bulbar failure decide on their own; the gas is the last thing to change. NIV is unsafe when she cannot protect her airway. No succinylcholine in denervation, and expect autonomic swings at induction.

The flow that never stops

Answer
Why

Severe BPD is a heterogeneous lung with long time constants. A slow rate lets it empty, larger breaths and a longer Ti reach the slow units, and higher PEEP holds the malacic airways open. Target SpO₂ 92–95% for the pulmonary hypertension.
